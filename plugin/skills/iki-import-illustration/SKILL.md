# Iki Import Illustration (one finished picture → rigged `.iki`)

Turn one finished character picture into a rigged `.iki` that, at rest, is the
picture itself, pixel for pixel, and that blinks, gazes, lip-syncs, turns, nods
and tilts its head with its hair swaying behind it.

```
picture → view_illustration_grid (survey, zoomed views) → landmarks.json → view_illustration_grid (landmarks drawn)
        → cut_layers_from_illustration → auto_rig_from_layers → render_poses
```

Nothing is generated by default: every layer is cut out of the picture, and
what a layer hides when the rig moves it (the forehead under the bangs, the hair
behind the face, the neck behind the chin, the skin under each feature) is
filled in from the paint around it. There is no automatic detection: the cut
finds every part from **landmarks you read off the picture**, and it reports
numeric checks that name the landmark to move. The work is reading coordinates
carefully; most of this file is how.

## When to use

- The user has ONE finished, front-facing picture of a character and wants it
  rigged, animated, or turned into an Iki model.

## When NOT to use

- No art at all: "make me a character" → the **iki-character** skill.
- Art already split into layers, or a layered PSD → the editor's "Import layer
  set" / PSD import (`examples/editor`), or `auto_rig_from_layers` directly
  with role-named PNGs.

Some pictures this cannot do well. Check the survey (Step 1) against this list
and, on a match, **stop and tell the user plainly** what is wrong and what
picture would work, before reading a single landmark:

- **Not front-facing**: a three-quarter or profile view. The rig turns a front
  view; it cannot un-turn one.
- **A covered face**: a hand, a cup, a mask or a prop over the face or the mouth.
- **Hair over an eye**: an eye must be whole to blink, and a strand lying on
  the lash in the lash's colour cannot be split from it (Landmark reference,
  `eyes`); bangs across the brows and locks beside the eyes are fine.
- **A busy backdrop**: the backdrop must be transparent or ONE flat colour. A
  gradient, a pattern, a scene or a textured paper ground is refused by the cut
  (`… backdrop is not one flat colour …`). The user can give the same picture
  with a transparent background.
- **Arms and a full body**: the cut writes a bust (head, hair, neck and torso as
  one `body`). Arms raised or in front of the body stay painted on the torso and
  never move; a full-body picture leaves the face small (next point). A bust
  crop of the picture is what works.
- **A small face**: the picture is scaled to a canvas 1100 px wide. A face under
  about 250 px wide on that canvas gives each eye only a few pixels to cut, and
  the blink and the gaze come out as smudges.
- **A bust cropped too tight**: the rig sways the torso on a lattice that must
  fit between the chin and the bottom of the picture. Let `h` be the chin's
  row minus the eye row (the irises' centres): a picture whose bottom edge
  lies less than 1.2 `h` below the chin is refused at the rig
  (`INVALID: auto-rig: layer "body.png": the body is too short between its
hips … would fold …`), and nothing in the landmarks fixes it. Ask for a
  wider crop; never pad or stretch the picture yourself.

## Prerequisites

- **The tools**, from the `iki` MCP server the plugin bundles:
  `view_illustration_grid` and `cut_layers_from_illustration` (both need
  `@ikijs/mcp` **0.38.0** or later), `auto_rig_from_layers`, `probe_rig` and
  `render_poses`. In Claude Code they are plugin-scoped
  (`mcp__plugin_iki_iki__cut_layers_from_illustration`, …); Codex may use other
  prefixes, so resolve them by tool name. Look before anything else. **If
  `view_illustration_grid` and `cut_layers_from_illustration` are not listed,
  the server is older than 0.38.0**: tell the user that this skill needs
  `@ikijs/mcp` 0.38.0 or later and the plugin's bundled server is older, so the
  plugin needs updating (or, in an Iki checkout, build the working tree with
  `pnpm --filter @ikijs/mcp build` and drive `packages/mcp/dist/cli.js` over
  stdio as the **iki-character** skill's Prerequisites show). Do not try to cut
  the picture by other means.
- **A browser** (Chrome, Edge or Chromium) for `render_poses` (Step 9); set
  `CHROME_PATH` for a non-standard install.
- **A working directory of your choice under the project**, for example
  `iki-char/` (this file writes `iki-char/`; put yours in its place). The
  tools refuse to write outside the directory the MCP server runs in (the
  project), and every output folder must exist before the call:
  ```bash
  mkdir -p iki-char/grid iki-char/layers iki-char/renders iki-char/renders/close
  cp <the user's picture> iki-char/picture.png   # keep its own extension if JPEG or WebP
  ```
  Keep it out of version control (`iki-char/` is already gitignored in the
  `iki` repo; elsewhere add yours to `.gitignore` before the first run). The
  user's picture and everything cut from it are theirs: never commit them.

## The frame

Every coordinate in this skill, in the landmarks, on the grid views and in the
cut's and `probe_rig`'s output is **canvas px on a canvas 1100 px wide**: the
picture scaled to 1100 px wide, its height by its aspect. Origin top-left, x
right, y DOWN: x runs 0 to 1099 and y 0 to the height minus 1, so the canvas's
right edge is x 1099, not 1100 (a point past it is refused). A coordinate
names one pixel; on the grid view, a line labelled `v` runs along the left
(top) edge of pixel `v`, so the pixel just right of (below) the line labelled
450 is 450.
Boxes are `[x0, y0, x1, y1]`, both corners inside the box.

**`L` and `R` are the CHARACTER's left and right.** `eyes.L` is the eye on the
VIEWER's right, so it has the larger x; the same goes for `brows.L` and for a
part you name `tail_L`. Two keys are in the viewer's frame instead:
`mouth.cornerL` is the viewer's LEFT end of the mouth line (the smaller x), and
a custom part's `side: "left"` takes the hair at smaller x.

> Sanity rule: every `L` key has the LARGER x of its pair, except
> `mouth.cornerL`, which has the smaller. The cut refuses a swapped eye pair
> (`INVALID: landmarks.eyes.L.box centre x … is not right of …`) but cannot
> catch swapped brows.

## Procedure

### Step 1 — Survey

```jsonc
// view_illustration_grid
{ "imagePath": "iki-char/picture.png", "outputPath": "iki-char/grid/whole.png" }
```

Defaults: the whole canvas, zoom 1, step 50. The text result's second line
gives the canvas size (`… on the 1100x1320 canvas …`): note the height. Open
`whole.png` and check the "cannot do well" list above, the crop's depth below
the chin included (read the eye row and the chin's row off a zoom 2 view of
the head). Then note, roughly, the
boxes to zoom into: the head, each eye, each brow, the nose, the mouth, each
lock hanging in front of an ear, and the root of every tail or ponytail.
Decide which locks are side locks (Landmark reference, `sideHair`) and which
parts are custom parts (`customParts`).

### Step 2 — One zoomed view per part

**Never read a coordinate off the whole-canvas view.** One canvas px is one
output px there, and a 1 px error is a missed eyelash. Every landmark comes from
a zoomed view of its own part:

| Part                         | `zoom` | `step`  | `crop` (canvas px)                    |
| ---------------------------- | ------ | ------- | ------------------------------------- |
| the head, hair and shoulders | 2 to 3 | 20      | the whole head with its hair          |
| each eye                     | 4 to 6 | 10      | the eye with 20 to 30 px around it    |
| each brow                    | 4      | 10      | the brow with 15 px around it         |
| the nose, the mouth          | 6      | 5 or 10 | the feature with 15 to 20 px around   |
| each lock in front of an ear | 3 to 4 | 10      | the lock from the bangs to its tip    |
| each tail or ponytail root   | 3 to 4 | 10      | the knot or ribbon and the hair round |
| the neck and the clothes     | 2      | 20      | the chin down to the canvas's bottom  |

```jsonc
// view_illustration_grid
{
  "imagePath": "iki-char/picture.png",
  "outputPath": "iki-char/grid/eye-L.png",
  "crop": { "x": 580, "y": 400, "w": 120, "h": 100 },
  "zoom": 6,
  "step": 10,
}
```

Keep each view under about 1500 px a side (`w` × `zoom`, `h` × `zoom`; split a
bigger crop into two views): a bigger image is shrunk when you look at it,
which undoes the zoom. The labels along the top and left edges are canvas px;
count from the nearest labelled line.

### Step 4 — Check the landmarks drawn over the picture, before cutting

```jsonc
// view_illustration_grid, with the landmarks.json contents as `landmarks`
{
  "imagePath": "iki-char/picture.png",
  "outputPath": "iki-char/grid/check-eye-L.png",
  "crop": { "x": 580, "y": 400, "w": 120, "h": 100 },
  "zoom": 6,
  "step": 10,
  "landmarks": { "…": "the whole landmarks object" },
}
```

Each shape is drawn in its key's colour between a dark and a light stroke, so it
reads over any paint, and labelled with its key (`eyes.L.iris`, `skinSeeds[2]`).
Go part by part at the same zooms as Step 2: does each eye box hold the whole
eye with its lash wings and lower lashes, does each iris ellipse sit on the
disc, does each brow line run along its brow's middle, are the mouth corners
on the line's ends, does `frontHair` run along the parting and the strands'
own edges, does each `sideHair` polygon hug its lock from inside `frontHair`
down to its tip, does `skull` stay inside the ears, does every seed
and sample sit on flat paint of its class? Then one whole-head view (zoom 2,
step 20) for the polygons. Fix what is off now: this costs nothing and catches
most errors before a cut.

### Step 5 — Cut

```jsonc
// cut_layers_from_illustration
{
  "imagePath": "iki-char/picture.png",
  "outputDir": "iki-char/layers",
  "landmarks": { "…": "the whole landmarks object" },
}
```

`INVALID: landmarks.<path> …` means the landmarks broke a rule (a missing key,
a point off the canvas, `cornerL` not left of `cornerR`, a swapped eye pair):
the message names the field; fix it and call again. A refusal writes nothing.

The text result lists the written files (role layers, then `custom/<id>.png`
per custom part, then `preview-labels.png` and `preview-landmarks.png`), then
one line per check, `CHECK <id>: pass|WARN|FAIL <value> (limit <limit>) <hint>`,
then the `MEASURE:` lines, and last `CUT pass` or `CUT FAIL: <failed check ids>`.

Every round, do all three:

1. **Read every `CHECK` line and the last line** (The checks, below). Every hint
   names the landmark to move, often with the numbers.
2. **Read `iki-char/layers/preview-labels.png`**: each label tinted over the
   picture at 60 %: peach = face, red = front hair, orange = a side lock,
   purple = back hair, blue = body, green = the features lifted off the face (eyes, brows, nose, mouth),
   and the custom parts in order cyan, green, yellow, magenta (a second custom
   part's green is close to the features': tell them apart by where they are).
   Untinted means keyed as backdrop, in no layer. It is canvas-sized, so look
   at it once whole, then through `view_illustration_grid` (its `imagePath`,
   the same canvas px) at zoom 3 to 4 on the places that go wrong: each ear,
   each eye's corners, both shoulders, and every place the hair meets the
   neck or the clothes. Every painted pixel takes some label (paint no flood
   reached takes its nearest one), so look for paint under the wrong colour:

   | you see                                                                                      | it means                                                                                                      | do                                                                                                                   |
   | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
   | a shoulder's or a garment's edge tinted hair                                                 | its outline touches the hair and went with it: it rides the head and shows as a ghost line on a turn          | a `bodyOnly` polygon round that stretch of outline, clear of the hair                                                |
   | a whole garment tinted hair                                                                  | its colour is nearer the hair samples                                                                         | a `bodyOnly` polygon over the garment                                                                                |
   | hair tips beside the neck tinted body                                                        | a tone no hair sample covers, or a thin tip lying on the clothes read as their outline: it will not move      | a `samples.hair` point on the tip (a piece holding a hair sample is never read as an outline); no `bodyOnly` over it |
   | an ear, or an ear lobe, tinted hair (purple, or orange inside a side lock's polygon) or body | no skin seed reaches it (a line or a lock cuts it off from the face)                                          | a `skinSeeds` point on it, one per piece; `skull` stays off the ear                                                  |
   | a lock in front of an ear tinted purple                                                      | it is not in a `sideHair` polygon: on a turn the face slides over it and the ear behind it reads as an island | a `sideHair` polygon round it (Landmark reference)                                                                   |
   | back hair beside a lock tinted orange                                                        | the `sideHair` polygon is wider than the lock: that hair will ride the face                                   | trace the polygon closer to the lock                                                                                 |
   | skin tinted hair, or hair tinted face                                                        | a sample on a line or on the other class's paint, or a tone no sample covers                                  | move that sample onto flat paint (`seeds` may name it), or add one on the tone                                       |
   | a tail or ponytail tinted purple                                                             | it is not cut out as a custom part, or its `line` / `polygon` misses it                                       | a `customParts` entry, or move its line                                                                              |
   | backdrop tinted anything                                                                     | backdrop the figure encloses (between the neck and a tail, under an arm), which the border's flood missed     | its point (`pockets` names it) in `backgroundSeeds`                                                                  |
   | figure paint left untinted                                                                   | paint keyed as a gap between strands                                                                          | a point inside it in `paintSeeds` (`pockets` names the keyed points)                                                 |

3. **Look at the eye layers** as Landmark reference, `eyes`, "Checking the
   eye layers" says: every round, and after any change to an eye landmark.

### Step 6 — Fix and re-cut: at most three rounds

Move the landmarks the hints name, add the optional keys a check asks for, view
the changed landmarks (Step 4), and cut again into the same `outputDir` (files
are overwritten; a role this cut no longer writes is removed).

**A hint can chase the wrong thing.** It reads paint, not intent: a hair wisp
or a crease touching an eye box, a hair outline along a brow. Before obeying a
hint, view the place it names (its x and y) at zoom 6 or 8 and check that what
it describes is the part. If you followed the same hint twice and it asks
again, stop following it: look at the layer and the preview, decide, and say
in Step 10 which hint you left and why.

**At most three fix rounds after the first cut.** Then stop fixing, keep the
best cut that ends `CUT pass`, and report what still warns or fails rather than
looping. A cut that still ends `CUT FAIL` after three rounds is not rigged:
report the failing lines and the preview to the user.

`CUT pass` with `WARN` lines is normal (The checks, below).

### Step 7 — The mouth

A closed-mouth picture never drew the one thing lip-sync needs: the open mouth.
Two routes:

- **Keep the drawn mouth** (the default). The cut writes it as the `mouth`
  role, and the rig stretches it open on `ParamMouthOpenY` (as tall as 0.8 of
  its width, and a little wider). Free and exactly the picture at rest; fine for a portrait, a blurred
  band when it talks.
- **Generate a lip set.** Draw `mouth_keyed.png` (an open mouth, its inside
  flat `#00FF00`) and `mouth_interior.png` (the cavity, teeth and tongue alone)
  and pass both to the cut, which places the set on the drawn corners: the
  mouth then folds open like the eyelid and lip-syncs cleanly. The cost: the
  closed mouth at rest becomes the generated line, not the drawn one, and the
  drawing **sends the user's picture to the image service** and may be billed.

**Ask the user before generating**, in plain words, for example: "Your picture
has a closed mouth, so there is no open mouth for talking. I can have an image
model draw one to match your picture: that sends your picture to the image
service and may cost money, and the mouth at rest becomes the drawn-in line
instead of yours. Or I keep your mouth and stretch it open, which is free but
looks blurry when talking. Which do you prefer?" If they decline or do not
answer, keep the drawn mouth. (Asking at the start lets the images draw while
you place landmarks.)

To generate: run `gen-images.sh` from the **iki-create-image** skill's
directory (`skills/iki-create-image/` in the plugin) with the user's picture
as `--ref`, two variants of each file, into `iki-char/parts/`
(`mkdir -p iki-char/parts` first). The prompts are the two quoted bullets
`mouth_keyed.png` and `mouth_interior.png` in the **iki-character** skill's
Step 1: open that file, copy both verbatim, and set `<STYLE>` to the picture's
own drawing style, hair and skin colours. Pick the keyed mouth whose green is
flat and even, whose outline closes all round and whose upper line is of
medium weight and ends at the corners; pick the interior with the least lip:

```bash
gen-images.sh --ref iki-char/picture.png iki-char/parts \
  "<mouth_keyed prompt>::mouth_keyed-a.png" "<mouth_keyed prompt>::mouth_keyed-b.png" \
  "<mouth_interior prompt>::mouth_interior-a.png" "<mouth_interior prompt>::mouth_interior-b.png"
```

Copy the picked variants to `iki-char/parts/mouth_keyed.png` and
`iki-char/parts/mouth_interior.png`, then re-cut with

```jsonc
  "mouthKeyed": "iki-char/parts/mouth_keyed.png",
  "mouthInterior": "iki-char/parts/mouth_interior.png",
```

added to Step 5's call. The cut writes `mouth_inner`, `lip_lower`, `lip_upper`
(and `mouth_teeth` / `mouth_tongue` when the interior has light paint) instead
of `mouth`, scaled so the set's corners land on `mouth.cornerL` and
`mouth.cornerR`, and adds a `MEASURE: lips: opening …` line. A key the composer
refuses comes back as `INVALID: …` naming the file: regenerate that one. Corners
too close for the set's outline to close are refused naming the least distance
that works.

### Step 8 — Rig

```jsonc
// auto_rig_from_layers
{
  "layers": [
    { "path": "iki-char/layers/hair_back.png" },
    { "path": "iki-char/layers/body.png" },
    { "path": "iki-char/layers/face.png" },
    // … every role PNG the cut listed, nothing under custom/ and no preview*.png
    { "path": "iki-char/layers/hair_front.png" },
  ],
  "outputPath": "iki-char/character.iki",
  "quantizeColors": 256,
  // only with custom parts, one entry per part, tie = its landmark's tie:
  "customParts": [
    {
      "id": "tail_L",
      "path": "iki-char/layers/custom/tail_L.png",
      "anchor": "hair_back",
      "tie": [800, 200],
      "after": "hair_back",
    },
  ],
}
```

Pass exactly the role layers the latest cut listed, the side locks
(`hair_side_L`, `hair_side_R`) among them: a feature that was not found writes
no layer, and passing a stale file is refused or wrong. Leave
`rigPlan`, `turnTargets` and `patches` out.

**What the text result shows**, in order: the `.iki` path; the `RIGPLAN:`
line; the fold report (`FOLD …` lines or `FOLDS: none over N poses`, then
`FOLDS expected: …`); the `PHYSICS` lines; with side locks and a nose, the
`HAIRSIDE` lines; with custom parts, the `CUSTOM`, `BAKE` and gate lines.
`HAIRSIDE cover <role>` says how far the lock's outer edge lies outside the
head's edge at the turn and at rest (negative: the turn bares cheek the lock
hides at rest), and `HAIRSIDE tail <role>` how far the eye and its lash lie
from the lock's face-side edge on the turn (negative: under the lock, as
intended). They are report-only: quote them in Step 10 and look at the ears
in the close-ups. It does **not** print the head's width or the turn the
rig settled on (`turn.achieved`, `turn.clamped`): those are only in the
structured result. What tells you the turn is cut down or the head mis-measured:

- before rigging, `frontHairWidth` passing, and no tail or side hair beside
  the head left purple on `preview-labels.png` (both widen the head the turn
  is fitted to);
- on the `RIGPLAN:` line, `turn.amount=<v> (requested 1, clamped at <v>)`: the
  art left the far eye too little room and the turn was cut down. Report it.
  With no `nose` layer no turn is fitted at all;
- after Step 9, the `SWEEP turn` lines and `turn/sheet.png`.

**`FOLD` lines** are report-only and never refuse the rig. `FOLDS expected:`
lists the closures the rig draws on purpose (an eye or the mouth shut): not
findings. A `FOLD flip …` or `FOLD collapse …` line names the part, its
`pose [<settings>]` and where it rests in model px (canvas px). One whose only
pose is the stress pose (`ParamAngleX`, `ParamAngleY` and `ParamAngleZ` all at
-30, the mouth open, the brows apart) is the extremes stacked: report it to
the user, do not chase it. Any other: render that pose with a crop round the
point it names (Step 9) and report what shows.

#### The bake (custom parts only)

**No custom parts: skip the bake, go to Step 9.** An unbaked custom part does
not move with the head: the head turns away from it. The simplest bake that
passes the gate is:

1. Rig once with the arguments above (the `customParts` included) but
   **without** `bakes`, to `iki-char/bare.iki`. Its gate fails; that is
   expected, and this model is only for measuring.
2. One `probe_rig` call on `iki-char/bare.iki` with
   `"outputPath": "iki-char/bare.probe.json"` and 16 queries, each
   `{ "op": "displacement", "part": "hair_back", "points": [<every part's tie>], "pose": { <one parameter>: <value> } }`
   for `ParamAngleX` at -30, -15, 15, 30; `ParamAngleY` at -30, -15, 15, 30;
   `ParamAngleZ` at -30, -20, -10, 10, 20, 30; and `ParamHairBackSway` at -20
   and 20. Keep that list of (parameter, value) in your script: the file is a
   JSON array of `{ op, part, points }`, one per query **in query order**,
   without the pose. Each `points` entry is the back hair's `[dx, dy]` at that
   tie. A `null` means the tie is not on the back hair: move it onto the
   head's hair at the knot.
3. Write `bakes` (a short Node script reading `bare.probe.json` is easiest).
   Per part and per head parameter, one target on a 1 column × 6 row lattice,
   whose 14 control points (2 per row, rows top to bottom, row-major) ALL take
   the displacement measured at that part's tie at that stop: the part then
   hangs from its knot and stays plumb on a roll instead of swinging out with
   the head. Plus one `ParamHairBackSway` target per part at -20 and 20: row
   `r` (0 at the top to 6 at the tip) takes the measured `[dx, dy]` plus a
   sideways `(value / 20) × (h / 30) × (r / 6)²` px on `dx`, `h` the part's
   height in px (its paint's top to its tip): a swing that grows with the
   square of the depth, a thirtieth of the part's height at the tip. No target
   takes a stop at 0: rest is implicit, and a stop there is refused.
   ```jsonc
   "bakes": { "targets": [
     { "id": "tail_L-ParamAngleX", "param": "ParamAngleX", "parts": ["tail_L"],
       "grid": { "cols": 1, "rows": 6 },
       "stops": { "-30": [[dx, dy], … 14 times], "-15": […], "15": […], "30": […] } },
     // … ParamAngleY, ParamAngleZ, then the ParamHairBackSway target, for every part
   ] }
   ```
4. Rig again with the same arguments plus `bakes`, to `iki-char/character.iki`.
   Each part prints `BAKE` lines and the gate's `CUSTOM <id> …` lines; the
   text ends `CUSTOM GATE pass` or `CUSTOM GATE FAIL: …`.

The gate's lines, per part: `FOLDS` (its least triangle area ratio), `TIE`
(the tie staying on the back hair where the head does not turn), `SLIDE` (the
same where it turns), `EXPOSED` (the art it hides showing), `TIP` (where its
free end lands, report-only). `FOLDS`, `TIE` and `SLIDE` must pass. On a part
cut from a picture, `EXPOSED` often fails: the back hair filled in under the
part's root shows at the most extreme poses (a full roll with a full swing),
where the drawing had nothing to show. That is acceptable: report it to the
user and do not chase it. A gate that throws
(`CUSTOM GATE FAIL: gate error: …`) or an `INVALID: BAKE ERROR …` is a package
bug: report it verbatim. The **iki-character** skill's Step 3 and the
**iki-character-artist** agent's "Baking the custom parts" go deeper (a far
tail going behind the head, rigid parts).

## Landmark reference

In filling order. "Off" says what goes wrong when it is placed wrong.

### `samples`

`{ "hair": { "base", "shadow"?, "highlight"? }, "skin": { "base", "shadow"? } }`,
each a list of `[x, y]` points (base: 1 or more; at most 32 points per class). A
pixel is hair or skin by which set its colour is nearer, so these decide the
whole cut. Each point reads a 5 × 5 px patch: keep it 3 px or more from any line.

- `hair.base`: two or three points on flat mid-tone hair, spread over the head,
  away from lines and strand edges.
- `hair.shadow`: on the darkest flat shading of the hair.
- `hair.highlight`: on the sheen, if the hair has one. A pale sheen can lie
  near a pale garment's colour and pull its shading into the hair (cloth
  shading tinted hair on `preview-labels.png`): drop or move the highlight
  point, and put a `bodyOnly` polygon over the cloth.
- `skin.base`: a cheek and the forehead, away from the blush.
- `skin.shadow`: the shade under the bangs, or under the chin on the neck.

Never on a line, never on a blush. To check a point, view it at zoom 8, step
5: its 5 × 5 patch is one grid cell and must be one flat colour. `seeds` is
the test the cut runs: a bad point fails it as `<path> [x, y] reads as <class>
to the other samples: …` or `… is on the backdrop …`. Off: a wrong sample is
the commonest cause of hair and skin swapping, across the whole picture.

### `eyes.L`, `eyes.R`

`{ "box": [x0, y0, x1, y1], "iris": { "cx", "cy", "rx", "ry" } }`.

- `box`: the whole eye drawing with 2 to 4 px to spare on every side: the lash
  wing's tip, the lower lashes, the lower lid line. Not the brow, not hair.
  The boxes `opening`'s hints suggest leave the same 2 to 4 px. Off: a box that
  clips the eye leaves the clipped paint on the face and tears the lash on a
  blink (`opening` fails and says how far to grow it); a loose box takes the
  shading round the eye into it (`opening` warns).

  **Strands at the box's edge.** A hair wisp or a line that crosses into the
  box without touching the eye's ink is left out of the eye by itself: never
  grow a box along one. One that touches the eye's ink (a strand lying on the
  lash wing) is not: it is cut into the eye layers and folds with the lash on
  a blink, and no check says so. Where the hair is as dark as the lash, the
  tool cannot tell them apart at all: everything in the box that is not skin
  is the eye's. It shows as hair-shaped pieces in the eye layers (below).
  Pulling the box in off
  such a strand clips the eye (the strand now counts as eye), so `opening`
  turns from WARN to FAIL and asks for the box back: keep the box that passes
  and report the strand. When `opening` warns that an edge cuts a stroke it
  cannot tell from the eye, look: hair or a line, leave that edge; the eye's
  own lash wing, grow as the hint says.

- `iris`: the full coloured disc as an ellipse, INCLUDING the part hidden under
  the upper lash. Place it by eye on the disc first. `cy` is the disc's widest
  row; `rx` is half that row's width; `ry` runs from `cy` down to the disc's
  bottom edge, not to the lower lid line, which must stay outside the ellipse.
  Its centre must lie inside `box`. A disc drawn squarer than an ellipse gives
  its corners to the iris by itself (iris-coloured paint up to a quarter of the
  radius past the ellipse): do not enlarge the ellipse to catch them. `iris`'s
  hint (the disc's x span, how far down it reaches, which way to move) is a
  suggestion to verify, not an order: it can chase the lash, and the check can
  then pass on a worse cut. Off: an ellipse wider than the disc or up in the
  lash pulls white or lash into the iris; one off the disc can make an open eye
  read as drawn closed (`iris` and `eye` warn).

**Checking the eye layers.** The checks passing is not proof. View
`eye_<side>.png`, `iris_<side>.png`, `lash_<side>.png` and
`lash_lower_<side>.png` with `view_illustration_grid`, one call per PNG at the
same crop and zoom as the eye's view (they are canvas-sized, so the
coordinates match). The sclera should be the eye's whole shape, filled white
under the iris; the iris a full disc; the lash the upper lid's dark ink only;
the lower lash the lower lid's ink. A bad iris fit shows as a dark,
lash-coloured band across the top of the iris layer, or as iris-coloured paint
left in the white or the lower lash: move the ellipse back by eye, and revert a
change that makes the layers worse even if the number improves. Hair-shaped
pieces in any of them are a strand at the box's edge (above). A dark ring line
drawn inside the iris along its bottom can land in the lower lash, and no
landmark fixes it: it shows on the gaze close-up as a dark arc left where the
iris was; report it.

### `brows.L`, `brows.R` (optional, each side)

`{ "line": [[x, y], …], "halfWidth"?, "tailHalfWidth"? }`. `line` runs along
the brow's middle, 2 or more points, **tail first** (the outer end, toward the
face's edge). `halfWidth` (default 8) is the half-width of the band the brow is
cut from; `tailHalfWidth` (default 5) the same at the tail, narrowing to it
over the first third of the line. Set both from the brow's own thickness,
read at zoom: `halfWidth` half its thickest part plus 1 px, `tailHalfWidth`
half the tail's plus 1 px (the cut takes a 2 px soft rim past the ink by
itself). A thin brow of 3 to 4 px wants `halfWidth` 3 and `tailHalfWidth` 2:
the defaults' 17 px band is far too wide for it. A brow mostly hidden behind
the bangs is cut as the short pieces that show between the strands; raised,
they go under the strands and mostly vanish: a limit to report. A brow drawn
in the hair's own colour where it meets the bangs cannot be told from them:
put the line on what shows, read `brow` and `browBand` there as less reliable,
look at `brow_<side>.png`, and report it. Off: a line
off the brow leaves it on the face (`brow` warns when the band holds none,
`browBand` when some of its ink lies past the band, saying where and how far
to move the line or widen the band); a band too wide takes hair or lash, and
where a strand crosses the brow, refills the strand far from its own hair
(`fills.hair_front.strands`). A brow left out stays painted on the face and
never moves.

### `nose` (optional)

`{ "box": [x0, y0, x1, y1] }` round the drawn nose marks with a few px to spare.
Off: a box that clips the nose leaves paint on the face. Left out, the nose
stays painted on the face, `MEASURE: nose: missing …` follows, and the head
turn loses its strongest depth cue.

### `mouth`

`{ "box": [x0, y0, x1, y1], "cornerL": [x, y], "cornerR": [x, y] }`. `box`: the
whole drawn mouth with a few px to spare. `cornerL` / `cornerR`: the two ends
of the mouth line, `cornerL` the VIEWER's left (smaller x); both inside `box`.
Off: a box that clips the mouth leaves paint on the face; a box on bare skin
fails `mouth`; with a lip set, a corner off the line's end skews the mouth.

### `frontHair`

A polygon (3 or more points) round the bangs and the strands that hang in
front of the face, from the parting at the top. Every hair pixel inside it is
`hair_front`; the rest of the head's hair is back hair, except what a
`sideHair` polygon takes. Run its edges where the hair itself divides: along
the parting, or along a strand's own edge. Never run an edge straight down
through continuous hair (at the temple, say) or across a strand: the part
inside moves with the bangs and the part outside with the back hair, so that
edge opens as a seam on a turn. Take a strand whole down to its tip or leave
it out whole. Hair hanging in front of an ear is a side lock (`sideHair`);
hair beside and behind the head is back hair (`customParts` says what that
means for long hair). A small clip or pin lying on the hair goes with the hair
round it (paint no flood claims takes its nearest label): keep it inside
`frontHair` if it sits on the bangs; it is not worth a custom part. Off:
`frontHairWidth` warns and the turn is clamped (The checks say what it
compares): move a side edge in to the next strand's edge. Narrower than the
bangs leaves their edge in the back hair.

### `sideHair.L`, `sideHair.R` (optional, each side)

A polygon (3 or more points) round a side lock: a lock that hangs from under
the bangs in FRONT of the ear and the cheek's edge (a bob's front locks), and
that is **distinct along its whole length**, with its own edges on both sides
down to its tip or to where it joins the hair below the ear. `L` is the
character's left, on the viewer's right. Its hair is `hair_side_<side>`, drawn
over the face and under `hair_front`, and it rides the face on a turn; left in
the back hair, it stays behind as the face slides, and the ear behind it reads
as a detached island. Hair that is part of a continuous mass beside the face
(wavy hair whose locks merge into the hair below) is back hair: a polygon edge
drawn through a mass shows as a straight tear on a turn, with the ear in
pieces round it. When in doubt, leave it in the back hair and report the ear
as a known limit. Placing it, in this order of priority:

1. Follow the lock's own edges, never a ruler line through hair: close round
   it (every hair pixel inside rides the face), down to where it really ends
   or joins the hair below, never sliced at a convenient row.
2. Start it up inside `frontHair` so the two overlap (the front hair keeps
   what both claim): the lock's top row needs the front hair right above it,
   and its root is then carried up to 12 px under the bangs. If its top lies
   outside `frontHair`, add a small wedge to `frontHair` over it.
3. Keep it off the eye's outer corner at rest (the rig slides that corner
   under the lock on a turn). If only an edge through the lock would do that,
   rule 1 wins: leave the lock in the back hair.
4. Lower `shellLower` on that side to where the lock ends, so there is back
   hair behind its lower end.

Thin wisps over an ear are not a side lock. One lying wholly on the ear is
taken into the face and stays painted on the ear; one crossing the ear's edge
stays in the back hair, and the ear shows its gap on a turn: report it.
Under the lock, skin is filled only inside `skull` and where the lock crosses
the face's own paint (the ear on one side, the cheek on the other): the ear
joins the face there by itself, but still needs its own `skinSeeds` point.
Off: `sideHair.<side>` warns (The checks).

### `skull`

A polygon (3 or more points): the head's skin as if bald, WITHOUT the ears.
Follow the face's own edge inside the ears, then continue it up under the
hair to a rounded top. It is where hidden skin is filled in: under the bangs,
and under a side lock where the lock lies on the cheek. Never draw it out over
an ear: that lays skin under a lock where the picture has hair behind it, and
shows as a pale patch on a turn (the ear joins the face under a lock by
itself, `sideHair`). Off: too small shows hair through the forehead on a
turn; far past the head's outline makes the fill up far from any skin
(`fills.face.forehead`).

### `shellLower`

A line, left to right (x strictly ascending, 2 or more points): where the back
of the head's hair ends behind the face. Hair is filled in behind the face and
the bangs down to it. Around the ear lobes for tied-up hair; lower, behind the
neck, for hair that hangs down; on a side with a side lock, down to where the
lock ends. Off: too high shows a gap under the jaw on a
turn.

### `skinSeeds`

Points (1 or more) on the face's skin: each cheek, the forehead, and **each
ear** (an ear is cut off from the face by the hair in front of it, and the
face is a flood from these points that lines stop). Skin no seed reaches is
left out of the face and takes the nearest other label. Off: a seed on hair, a line or the backdrop fails
`seeds`; a missing ear seed leaves the ear out of the face (look at
`preview-labels.png`).

### `bodySeeds`

Points (1 or more) on the neck and the clothes. The body is a flood from them
over every solid paint that is not hair-coloured (by the samples) and not the
face: it **crosses drawn lines**, trims and thin stripes, so one seed takes a
uniform of many panels joined through such paint, and seams need no seed of
their own. It stops at hair-coloured paint (a dark jacket, and with
near-black hair, the lines inked in the same black) and at the face: put one
seed in each piece such paint walls off, and a `bodyOnly` polygon over a
garment that is itself hair-coloured. A thin hair-coloured stripe or outline
on the clothes is given back to the body by itself; a wider band (a dark tie,
a belt) is not: `bodyOnly`. A seed reads one pixel: put it on flat cloth or
skin, a few px from any line. Off: paint no seed reaches stays out of the
body and takes the nearest other label, so it moves with the head or the hair
(Step 5's table).

### `neckTopY`

The canvas row the neck is carried up to behind the chin: the nose's bottom
row (`nose.box`'s `y1`), or with no nose, halfway between the irises' `cy` and
the chin's lowest row. The neck is drawn up there only behind the face and the
front hair, which hide it at rest; it must reach high enough that they still
hide its top when the head turns, nods and tilts. Off: too low shows a gap or
an edge under the jaw on a turn or a nod (Step 9's close-ups).

### `customParts` (optional)

Anything tied off at one point that hangs from the head and would widen it:
twin tails, a ponytail, big ribbons, a long braid. Long loose hair that hangs
from the whole head, behind the shoulders or in front of them, is not a
custom part: it is back hair, and `shellLower` sits lower, behind the neck.
Back hair is drawn behind the body, and the body has no paint under hair
lying over it: hair drawn over the shoulders or a collar slides under the
body's edge when it sways or the head turns, leaving pale streaks or empty
spots on the body. This is a known limit: report it. Each custom part is cut out of the back hair into
`custom/<id>.png` and rigged as a custom part (Step 8). **Left in the back hair
they make the rig measure the head far wider than it is (one pair of twin tails:
1.6 times), and the turn goes wrong.** A part's `id` is a plain identifier (letters, digits, `_`, not starting
with a digit) that no role takes, nor a part id a role yields (`elbow_L`,
`forearm_R`): `tail_L`, `tail_R`, `ponytail`. Two forms:

```json
{
  "id": "tail_R",
  "tie": [300, 200],
  "side": "left",
  "line": [
    [315, 170],
    [300, 200],
    [290, 260],
    [285, 340],
    [290, 420]
  ],
  "below": 400,
  "ribbonBox": [270, 150, 335, 225]
}
```

```json
{
  "id": "tail_L",
  "tie": [800, 200],
  "polygon": [
    [785, 170],
    [1000, 170],
    [1000, 900],
    [800, 900],
    [815, 420],
    [800, 260]
  ]
}
```

- **Side form** (`side`, `line`, `below`, `ribbonBox`?): `line` traces where
  the part leaves the head's hair, top to bottom (y strictly ascending, 2 or
  more points); the back hair on `side` of it (`"left"`: smaller x, as the
  viewer sees it) is the part. Under the line's last point, the part is
  everything on that side of the column x = `below`: set it between the part
  and the hair or body beside it. `ribbonBox` is a box round a ribbon or tie at
  the knot, whose coloured paint then goes with the part.
- **Polygon form**: the back hair inside `polygon` is the part. Simpler when
  the part hangs clear of everything else.
- `tie`: the knot, where the part hangs from the head; the part swings about it,
  and Step 8 passes it to the rig, which needs it inside both the part's paint
  and the back hair's (the cut fills back hair in under the root for this). Put
  it on the hair at the root, a few px inside the part. Off: a tie off the root
  tears the part from the head as it swings.

Off: a line or polygon that misses a strand leaves it in the back hair (where
it widens the head); one inside the part leaves its root in the back hair
(`fills.custom.<id>.root` warns when the hair under the root is made up far
from any hair).

### `backgroundSeeds`, `paintSeeds`, `bodyOnly` (only when a check asks)

- `backgroundSeeds`: points in backdrop the figure encloses (a gap between the
  neck and a tail), which the border's flood cannot reach. Add the points the
  `pockets` hint names for `backgroundSeeds` when they are backdrop showing
  through; the cut accepts every one. A region the hint calls pale paint near
  the backdrop's colour is paint and needs nothing.
- `paintSeeds`: points in backdrop-coloured paint inside the hair that is paint,
  not backdrop (a pale clip between two locks). Add one when `pockets` lists a
  point it keyed by itself that is really paint.
- `bodyOnly`: polygons (3 or more points each) where paint the samples call hair
  belongs to the body: a dark jacket under near-black hair, a hair-coloured bow
  on the chest. Add one when `seeds` says a body seed reads as hair, or when
  `preview-labels.png` shows clothes tinted as hair.

print together, `L` first, then each brow's `brow` and `browBand`; a custom

`frontHairWidth`). Levels: a `fail`-level check that does not pass makes
the cut `CUT FAIL`; a `warn`-level one prints `WARN` and the cut still passes.
A limit of `none` means the check only reports.

**The last line.** `CUT pass` means no fail-level check failed; `CUT FAIL:
<ids>` lists the ones that did. `CUT pass` with `WARN` lines is normal. On most
pictures expect:

- `fills.face.forehead` under heavy bangs: the forehead is mostly hidden, so
  little skin is there to fill from. Check `skull` and the forehead seeds once;
  if it still warns, it is the picture.
- `pockets` whenever it keyed gaps between strands by itself (it always warns
  then): look at each listed point on `preview-labels.png`, and only act when
  one is really paint.
- `eye.L` / `eye.R` on a tinted or heavily shaded eye white, when the eye layer
  looks right.

Every other `WARN` deserves one look and, usually, the fix in the table.

**`MEASURE:` lines** are the layer-geometry checks a cut can act on, after the
checks. `MEASURE: all geometry checks passed` when there is nothing to say.
Otherwise:

- `nose: missing — …`, `hair_back: missing — …`, `body: missing — …`: that
  role was not written. Add the `nose` box (or accept a turn with no nose
  lead); a missing `hair_back` or `body` means the samples or seeds missed it.
- `lips: opening W px wide, H px tall at its centre under a L px line — …`
  (with a lip set): the opening the rig reads. Report it.
- With side locks, one line per problem, each naming the landmark to move:
  `hair_side_<side>: needs hair_front …`, a root showing past the bangs and
  the lock over its eye's outer corner (fix as for `sideHair.<side>`), and
  `… the lock leans inward about N px and leaves bare pixels …` (nothing is
  painted behind the lock there on a turn: lower `shellLower` under it).
- Anything else (a lip set the rig would refuse, a lash whose top row is off
  its sclera's, `the written layers could not be measured`) is a fault in the
  cut, not in your landmarks: report it verbatim and do not rig on it.

## Pitfalls

- **A brow drawn through the bangs** is fine: the strand over it is refilled
  and the brow passes under it. Keep the band no wider than the brow.
- **Never pad, stretch or repaint the picture** to get past a refusal: tell
  the user what the picture needs.
- **A brow drawn right out to the face's edge shows over the side hair on a
  wide turn**: the rig moves a brow further than the face's edge, a known
  limit of the rig. It shows in the `turn-m30` / `turn-p30` close-ups as a pale
  patch at the temple; report it to the user, and do not move the brow line
  for it (that only leaves the tail on the face).
- **Ghosts the rig leaves**, known limits to report, not chase: the chin's line
  can leave a ghost on the neck when the head nods up, and an ear's outline a
  dotted ghost on the back hair on a turn.
- **The face has no outline where hair hid it.** Where side strands covered
  the face's edge, the cut fills skin under them but has no line to draw, so on
  a big turn that edge shows soft. Report it; do not chase it.
- **A picture drawn with the mouth open** has no closed mouth: the drawn route
  rests open. Tell the user before cutting.
- **Never use art the user has no right to use.** A picture of someone else's
  character or someone else's drawing is theirs, not the user's: ask whose it
  is when unsure, and never send it to the image service without that answer.
