The front hair turns with the face as one piece, its crown included, while the back hair holds only the cap's edges. On the turn the cap's blend toward the back hair's motion is now per vertex column: it holds each bound edge of a cap row (one its back hair stops holding on the nod — a twin-tail back hair's notch beside the dome, a tucked temple) and falls in toward the axis as fast as each cell can lean, so the bangs over the face keep riding the face plate. Before, one such edge held its whole row, the bangs over the face included, and the ramp below it carried that lag down to the eyes' top: on a twin-tail set the cap stayed nearly still and the bang tips moved 0.6 of the face. The crown (above the face plate's top) now turns as the cap's top row, column by column, instead of easing to the back hair's motion toward its top, so nothing leans between crown and cap and the parting moves with the face; it binds nothing of its own, so where the back hair above it is thinner than its slide its top edge draws the head's top outline.
