# Corpus

Committed part layers for five characters, used as a regression baseline for the auto-rig. Each character has `layers/*.png` (the role-named parts), `rig-plan.json` and, when a character has accepted patches, `patches.json` (none does today). A character with custom parts also has `custom-parts.json`, `custom/*.png` and `bakes.json`.

- `pnpm corpus` re-rigs every character, rewrites `examples/playground/public/<name>.iki` (bob's is `hero.iki`, the site's hero) and `corpus/<name>/metrics.json`.
- `pnpm corpus:check` re-rigs into `.corpus-check/` and diffs against the committed files.

The rig options are the ones the character loop rigs with: `quantizeColors` 256, `rigPlan` from `rig-plan.json` (`{}` for all five, the profile's defaults), `patches` from `patches.json`, `customParts` from `custom-parts.json` and `bakes` from `bakes.json`, through the same `autoRigFromLayers` path as the MCP `auto_rig_from_layers` tool. All five draw the lip-set mouth, the blush (`shy`, `ParamCheek`), lower lashes and both hair layers (hair physics).

`mint` stays as the same character WITHOUT the side locks: the guard that a layer set without the role rigs byte-for-byte as before. `mint-locks` is the same layers plus `hair_side_L`/`hair_side_R`; its `metrics.json` also records `turn.hairSide` (the lock's cover and tail numbers, report-only: no bound reads them yet, so a later slice can design bounds from real numbers).

When first committed, the mint and silver-hood baselines are byte-identical to the models the character loop last accepted for them. Bob's is a new baseline: the lip-set layers plus the hero's blush, a combination no earlier model was rigged from (its last loop rig, `bob-lips2-parab.iki`, had no blush and no `quantizeColors`).

Mint's four `eyeWarp_R` patches were dropped on 2026-10-30: they existed only at `ParamAngleX` −30 with `ParamAngleY` −30 and 0, so a nod with the head turned slid the far eye in and out (the gap between the eyes breathed by about 12 px); with the eyes made smaller the far eye clears the ear without them. The corpus no longer replays a patch; the patch layer's own tests cover it.

The legacy `mouth.png` + `mouth_open.png` crossfade is deliberately not covered; no committed model uses it.

`twin-tails` guards custom parts: that they rig, that baked lattices still move them as the engine lands them, and that the per-part gate passes. Its `layers/` are twin's role layers; `hair_back.png` is the head's hair alone (a re-cut that replaced the flat filler band with an ellipse fill), and the two tails are not roles, so they sit apart in `custom/tail_R.png` and `custom/tail_L.png`, canvas-sized PNGs. `custom-parts.json` lists them as `{ "customParts": [...] }`, each entry the tool's own (`id`, `anchor`, `tie`, `after`) with `file` in place of `path`, resolved under `custom/`; the order is the draw order. `bakes.json` is `{ "bakes": { "targets": [...] } }`: the tails' ten lattices (five parameters per tail) of experiment C's run2-turn, copied as data, and the hairpin's four (below). The tails' are data without the program that wrote them because the program was a one-off search over the experiment's own tools; what the corpus keeps is its output, which the gate reads on every rig. The tool's `BAKE ERROR` only compares what the engine lands with what the bake asked for (the closed form against the player), so a wrong stop in the data would bake quietly; `pnpm corpus:check`'s model hash and metrics diff are what catch a change. `metrics.json` gains `customParts`: the gate's verdict and each part's worst readings (the fold ratio, the tie, the slide and its distance to the anchor's paint, the exposed art, the tip's carry and travel). A failing gate makes `pnpm corpus` and `pnpm corpus:check` exit non-zero. The tails' lattices were measured on the older cut of `hair_back`; on this cut the tie's distance to the anchor's paint reads about 5.7 / 5.1 px (limit 6.0), the one number the re-cut changed. A changed `customParts` reading in `metrics.json` is the head moving under fixed lattices. If the gate fails, the bakes must be solved again for the new rig, or the rig change is a regression to fix; do not re-baseline, and re-running `pnpm corpus` cannot fix it, because the program that wrote the bakes is not kept. In practice the bakes are produced by the plugin's artist procedure: a bare rig, `probe_rig`, and a small program that writes `bakes.json`.

`twin-tails` also carries a rigid accessory, `custom/hairpin.png`: a gold star hairpin generated once (2026-11-14, one image job through the `iki-create-image` skill, `--ref iki-char/twin/reference.png`) on a transparent background. It was placed by a one-off step, since `auto_rig_from_layers` takes a canvas-sized PNG and the composer places no custom parts: pixels of alpha under 8 cleared, trimmed and scaled to 80x38 px (a fifth of the 402 px face), composited at +410+265 on a transparent 1100x1100 canvas with ImageMagick over the left side of the bangs, the bar lying along the hair, wholly on `hair_front`'s paint. Its entry is `anchor` and `after` `hair_front`, `tie` [428, 284] (the star's centre, opaque in both layers) and `rigid: true`. Its four targets in `bakes.json` are 1x1 lattices whose four points share `hair_front`'s displacement at the tie, read with `probe_rig` on the bare rig: `ParamAngleX` -30/-15/15/30, `ParamAngleY` -30/30, `ParamHairSwayX` and `ParamHairSwayZ` -20/20. There is none for `ParamAngleZ`: unbaked it rolls with the head, as the bangs do. The gate reads its `RIGID` bend as 0.0 px.
